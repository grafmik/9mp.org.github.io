Her story

Born here. Still here.

Apinya grew up three streets from Hua Hin's fishing pier, back when the tallest thing on the beachfront was a coconut palm.

She read business at Chulalongkorn, spent four years in commercial leasing in Bangkok, and came home in 2013 with a licence and a strong opinion about how property was being sold on the coast — too fast, too loud, and too often to people who had not been told what they were actually buying.

Twelve years later the practice is still deliberately small. She takes on a handful of buyers at a time so that each one gets the same thing: a person who picks up the phone, who has met the juristic manager, and who will tell you plainly when a building is a bad idea.

Before you ask

The questions everyone has

Can a foreigner actually own property in Thailand?

A condominium, yes — outright and in your own name, provided the building's foreign quota (49% of saleable area) has room. Land and houses work differently, usually through a 30-year registered lease or a Thai company structure. Apinya will walk you through which route applies to the specific property, and her lawyer confirms it in writing before any deposit.

What does the whole process cost beyond the price?

Budget roughly 2–3% of the purchase price for transfer fees, stamp duty and withholding tax combined — usually split with the seller by negotiation. Legal fees for an independent title check run ฿25,000–฿60,000. There is no buyer's commission: Apinya is paid by the seller.

Can I buy without flying to Thailand?

Yes, and many clients do. The process is a live video walkthrough, a full photo and defect set, an independent lawyer's title report, and a power of attorney for the land office transfer. Nothing is signed until you have the lawyer's report in hand.

What rental return is realistic?

For a well-located beachfront one or two bedroom under professional management, 4–6% net is a fair expectation, with high season carrying most of it. Anyone promising you 8–10% guaranteed is quoting a marketing programme, not a market.

Which languages does Apinya work in?

Thai and English, both fluently, in writing and in person. She works with trusted interpreters for German, French and Russian clients where a contract detail needs to be exact.
